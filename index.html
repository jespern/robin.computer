05 / Good ideas have a lineage

Modern intelligence.
Old-school discipline.

qmail, for separation.

Robin’s process design is heavily inspired by qmail: small processes with narrow jobs and only the access they need. Keeping untrusted mail, credentials and model work apart helps limit what a failure or malicious message can reach.

OTP, for recovery.

Robin takes inspiration from Erlang’s OTP through Suture, bundled with its Go code. Supervised services can recover from failures, so a recoverable worker can restart without taking healthy neighbours with it. The benefit is an assistant built to keep going.

06 / Personal, by design

Close to your life.
Careful with its reach.

Robin is built for one person, running on their own infrastructure. A continuous conversation, with your context behind it.

Your state stays yours.

Mail, calendars, conversation and decision history live on your host. Model requests go to the configured vendor; self-hosted does not mean local-only inference.

Reading isn’t permission to act.

Mail triage runs in a fresh context with no tools. The conversation agent gets a restricted read bridge. Write actions belong to a separate, approval-gated actuator in the design.

A reason you can come back to.

Robin records how a decision was made. The audit view and replay tools let you inspect it, instead of guessing what the assistant was thinking.

In active development
